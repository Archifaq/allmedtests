# Category sources review

## allergy-sensitivity (us/uk/ie)

- Source 1: MedlinePlus, "Allergy Blood Test", https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/allergy-blood-test/, accessed 2026-08-19.
  What was used: supports the general explanation that allergy blood tests measure IgE antibodies and can be used when allergy symptoms suggest testing may be appropriate.
- Source 2: NHS inform, "Allergies", https://www.nhsinform.scot/illnesses-and-conditions/immune-system/allergies/, accessed 2026-08-19.
  What was used: supports the overview of common allergy test types, including skin prick testing, blood tests, and patch tests.
- Source 3: American Academy of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology, "Allergy Testing", https://www.aaaai.org/tools-for-the-public/conditions-library/allergies/allergy-testing, accessed 2026-08-19.
  What was used: supports the framing that allergy testing is interpreted together with symptoms, medical history, and a clinical exam rather than as a standalone marketplace product.

## sti-testing (us/uk/ie)

- Source 1: CDC, "Getting Tested for STIs", https://www.cdc.gov/sti/testing/index.html, accessed 2026-08-19.
  What was used: supports the general description that STI testing can involve blood, urine, or swab samples and that many infections may not have symptoms.
- Source 2: CDC, "STI Screening Recommendations", https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/screening-recommendations.htm, accessed 2026-08-19.
  What was used: supports the broad list of STI topics commonly covered by screening guidance, including chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, HIV, hepatitis, and related infections.
- Source 3: NHS, "Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)", https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sexually-transmitted-infections-stis/, accessed 2026-08-19.
  What was used: supports the plain-language explanation that STIs are passed through sexual contact and that testing may use urine, blood, or swab samples.
